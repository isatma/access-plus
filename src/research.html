How the simulated data was made

Each virtual participant has an onset type, a rate of decline, a starting ALS functional score and a usual position. Every two weeks the model works out how much function has been lost, and how well each technology still works at that level of loss.

  • Onset: about 1 in 4 bulbar onset, in line with large trial databases (PRO-ACT)
  • Decline: functional score loss averages a little under 1 point a month, spread from slow to fast; bulbar onset declines faster
  • Pointing: head pointing about 2.5 bits per second and gaze pointing about 2.1 at the start, slowing as function is lost
  • Eye-gaze typing: about 8 words per minute, with wide variation between people
  • Technology tolerance: each method keeps working until a set amount of function is lost; voice goes first with bulbar onset, eye gaze and EMG last
  • Switching: a participant moves to the next method after two check-ins below 80% success
  • Spasticity and tremor: an index from 0 to 1 that grows with loss, scaled by each person's tendency

Sources for the figures used:

  • Perry et al., Muscle & Nerve 2021: PRO-ACT, 6,149 participants
  • PRO-ACT analysis of 3,203 patients: faster decline with bulbar onset and older age
  • Hansen et al. 2018: gaze, head and mouse pointing throughput
  • SpeakFaster Observer: 8.1 words per minute eye-gaze typing in ALS

The model is a teaching and planning tool. Its curves are plausible, not measured. Real participants' data will replace it once consent, secure storage and ethics approval are in place.
