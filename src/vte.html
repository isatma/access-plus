    Ready to try

    Pointing Environment

    Every target you reach plays the next note, so each level ends with a song. Nobody fails: if the timer runs out, the level simply ends and you move on.

    1. Warm-up Pond: five big lily pads, no timer
    2. Target Garden: one flower at a time
    3. Shrinking Stars: targets get smaller
    4. Floating Notes: targets drift slowly
    5. Melody Path: play Mary Had a Little Lamb in order
    6. Return Lap: Target Garden again, to compare first and last

    Use the way you already access a computer

    The environment adapts to the input, not the other way round. Try two methods in one visit and the rundown compares them side by side.

    Hand mouse or trackpadClick to select. A useful baseline for comparison.
    Eye trackerAny tracker that moves the pointer. Rest on a target until the ring fills; dwell time is adjustable.
    Your own head mouse or FaceNavUse the head or face tracking you already have, commercial or open-source, with dwell or its own click.
    Built-in nose trackingNo extra software: your webcam steers the pointer, processed on your own device.
    One switchTargets light up in turn; press when the right one lights. Scan speed is adjustable.
    Two switchesOne switch moves the light, the other selects, at your own pace.

    What it measures

    SpeedTime to reach each target, and targets per minute.
    AccuracyMisses, wrong targets and dwell slips (resting nearby without selecting).
    ThroughputA standard pointing score (Fitts' law, bits per second) that allows fair comparison across target sizes and distances.
    FatigueThe Return Lap repeats level two, so the change from first to last shows how pace holds up over a session.
    Ease and preferenceAfter each level: how easy it felt and how tired you are. At the end: which input you would choose.
    Your data, your choiceSaved to your test sessions in this browser and the Research workspace. Download a CSV of every target for analysis.

    For researchers: environments, components and experiments

    The VTE is built in layers so researchers can create and store their own studies. The Environment Editor lets anyone build a desktop of their own and share it; it runs on the Songboards engine, the Access+ music board.

    ComponentsBuilding blocks: targets, timers, scanners, sounds, rating questions and measures.
    ChallengesShort tasks assembled from components, such as one level of the Pointing Environment.
    EnvironmentsA themed set of challenges for one computer skill: pointing, scrolling, typing, driving.
    StudiesA study groups sessions under participant codes so they can be compared over time. Set one up on the research dashboard; the experiment builder for custom conditions comes next.
