Access+ · draft for review

Build assistive technology skills that carry over to everything you control.

For people with advanced and complex impairments, and the people who support them. Practice with the controls you use on computers, phones, wheelchairs and robotic arms, in short games and simulators. Your results build a skills profile that shows what works for you, and helps you move to new devices, hardware and software.

How it works

🎮Try

Pick a game or simulator and use the control you have, or one you are thinking about.

🎵Practice

Short levels with music and clear feedback. Go at your own pace, take breaks, try again.

📈Build your skills profile

Each game and simulator adds to a profile of speed, accuracy and comfort, so the skills you build on one device can guide the next.

Your AI agent

The AI agent assesses your needs, suggests the technology and sensors that fit you, and then helps you practice in the simulators.

  • 1Gets to know youAsks what you want to do and finds your most reliable way to control technology.
  • 2Follows you across devicesOne profile for your computer, phone, chair and home, and it notices early when your needs change.
  • 3You stay in controlEvery suggestion is explained, you decide, and your practice becomes evidence for your therapist and funders.

Virtual Training Environment · prototype

Practice computer skills as games, and see what works

Short, colorful environments for trying eye tracking, head and face tracking, and switches. Every target you reach plays a note, so each level ends in a song, and a plain-language rundown compares the inputs you tried. The Recreation lab of music and art apps from ISATMA lives here too.

Research and sciencePrototype · draft for discussion

See skills grow, and find what helps most

A research workspace for scientists that follows every measure inside every technology: accuracy, dwell time, stamina, spasticity and tremor, voice and more. Compare snapshots, follow the technology chain, spot early signs that settings or support may need a change, and see which equipment people own but rarely use.

  • For participants, clinicians and researchers
  • Filters, drill-downs and downloadable data files
  • Runs on generic, simulated data for discussion; not real people
